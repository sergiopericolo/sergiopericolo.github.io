Lorenz attractor

Seven trajectories start from points just \(10^{-3}\) apart: after a few loops they follow completely different paths, while staying confined to the same structure. Like a graph of notes: simple local rules, unpredictable global shape.

\[\dot{x} = \sigma (y - x), \qquad \dot{y} = x(\rho - z) - y, \qquad \dot{z} = xy - \beta z\]

Move the sliders and watch how the behaviour changes. With \(\rho < 1\) everything collapses to the origin; below the Hopf threshold (about \(24.74\) with the classic values) trajectories spiral into the two fixed points \(C^\pm\); above it, chaos appears. The Lyapunov exponent \(\lambda > 0\) measures how fast two nearby trajectories separate: \(\delta(t) \approx \delta_0 \, e^{\lambda t}\).
