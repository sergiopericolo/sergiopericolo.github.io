Simple and Compound Interest

en

Simple and Compound Interest

Lectures 2–3 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). The two workhorse regimes: interest proportional to time (simple) and interest on interest (compound). Then the tools to change the period of a rate.

Roadmap:

  1. Simple interest — linear accumulation, rational discount.
  2. Compound interest — exponential accumulation, separability.
  3. \(d < \delta < i\) — the order of the three rates.
  4. Mixed law — compound on whole years, simple on the fraction.
  5. Equivalent rates and nominal rates — from annual to monthly and back.

Simple interest

Interest earned in an interval is proportional to its length; the constant is the unit rate \(i\).

\[f(t) = 1 + it\]

\[M(t) = C(1 + it) = C + Cit, \qquad I(t) = Cit\]

The force of interest decreases over time:

\[\delta(t) = \frac{i}{1 + it}\]

\(\delta\) is not constant, so simple interest is not separable. Interest is always computed on the initial capital only. If the investor stops and restarts the operation, the interest already earned starts earning interest too: the final amount is higher than with one single investment.

Rational discount

Inverting the accumulation factor gives the rational discount factor:

\[v(t) = \frac{1}{1 + it}\]

\[D(t) = M - C = \frac{Mit}{1 + it}, \qquad d(t) = 1 - v(t) = \frac{it}{1 + it}\]

Compound interest

Interest earned in an interval is proportional to its length and to the amount accumulated at its start. The constant is the force of interest, now constant:

\[\frac{f'(t)}{f(t)} = \delta\]

So compound interest is separable. Its link with the unit rate is

\[e^\delta = 1 + i \quad\iff\quad \delta = \ln(1+i)\]

and the accumulation factor is

\[f(t) = e^{\delta t} = (1 + i)^t\]

\[M(t) = C(1 + i)^t, \qquad I(t) = C[(1 + i)^t - 1]\]

Discounting under compound interest

\[v(t) = e^{-\delta t} = (1+i)^{-t}, \qquad C(t) = M(1+i)^{-t}\]

\[D(t) = M[1 - (1+i)^{-t}], \qquad d(t) = 1 - (1+i)^{-t}\]

Separability, checked

\[f(t+h) = e^{\delta(t+h)} = e^{\delta t} \cdot e^{\delta h} = f(t) \cdot f(h)\]

The order of the three rates

From \(\delta = \ln(1+i)\) and the concavity of the logarithm, for every \(i > 0\):

\[d < \delta < i\]

  • \(\delta < i\): the log lies below its tangent at 1, so \(\ln(1+i) < i\).
  • \(\delta > d\): \(\ln(1+i) > \frac{i}{1+i}\), again by concavity.

The force of interest is always between the discount rate and the interest rate.

Mixed law (linear convention)

For non-integer durations we can use compound interest on the integer part \(n\) and simple interest on the remaining fraction \(t - n\):

\[M(t) = C(1+i)^n [1 + i(t-n)]\]

Inside each year the simple factor is above the compound one (see note 3), so the mixed law pays slightly more than pure compounding. It is not separable.

Equivalent rates

Two rates are equivalent if, applied to the same non-zero capital for the same non-zero time, they give the same final amount. Let \(i\) be the annual rate and \(i_h\) the rate for a fraction \(1/h\) of the year (\(h = 2\) semiannual, \(h = 4\) quarterly, \(h = 12\) monthly).

Simple interest — proportional:

\[i_h = \frac{i}{h}\]

Compound interest — from \((1+i)^t = (1+i_h)^{ht}\) at \(t = 1\):

\[1+i = (1+i_h)^h, \qquad i_h = (1+i)^{1/h} - 1\]

Nominal annual rate

The nominal annual rate convertible \(h\) times a year is

\[J_h = h \cdot i_h\]

  • Simple interest: \(J_h = i\) always.
  • Compound interest: \(J_h < i\) for every \(h > 1\).

Theorem. Under compound interest the sequence \(\{J_h\}\) is decreasing in \(h\) and bounded below. Its limit is the force of interest:

\[\lim_{h \to +\infty} J_h = \delta = \ln(1+i)\]

So \(\delta\) is the nominal annual rate convertible infinitely often. With \(i = 10\%\) the nominal rate falls from \(10\%\) toward \(\ln 1.1 \approx 9.53\%\):

Key takeaways

  • Simple: \(f(t) = 1 + it\), \(\delta(t) = i/(1+it)\) decreasing, not separable; rational discount \(v(t) = 1/(1+it)\).
  • Compound: \(f(t) = (1+i)^t = e^{\delta t}\), constant \(\delta = \ln(1+i)\), separable.
  • \(d < \delta < i\) for every \(i > 0\).
  • Mixed law: \(M = C(1+i)^n[1 + i(t-n)]\), not separable.
  • Equivalent rates: \(i_h = i/h\) (simple), \(i_h = (1+i)^{1/h} - 1\) (compound).
  • \(J_h = h i_h\); under compounding \(J_h < i\) and \(J_h \downarrow \delta\).
