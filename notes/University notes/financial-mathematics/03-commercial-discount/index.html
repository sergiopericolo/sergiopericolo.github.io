Commercial Discount and Average Rates

en

Commercial Discount and Average Rates

Lecture 4 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). The third regime, where the discount (not the interest) is proportional to time. Then a side-by-side comparison of the three regimes and the average rate of an operation with changing rates.

Roadmap:

  1. Commercial discount — the discount factor \(v(t) = 1 - dt\).
  2. Its accumulation factor — a hyperbola, not separable.
  3. Comparing the regimes — simple, compound, commercial on one chart.
  4. Variable rates and average rates.

Commercial discount (interest in advance)

In the commercial discount regime the discount earned between \(t\) and \(t+h\) is proportional to \(h\):

\[v(t) - v(t+h) = \alpha h, \qquad \alpha \ge 0\]

At \(t = 0\), \(h = 1\), with \(v(0) = 1\): \(1 - v(1) = \alpha\). So \(\alpha\) is the unit discount rate \(d\).

Divide by \(h\) and let \(h \to 0\):

\[\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{v(t+h) - v(t)}{h} = -d \implies v'(t) = -d\]

Integrate from \(0\) to \(t\):

\[v(t) = 1 - dt\]

The factor must stay positive, so the regime only works on a bounded horizon:

\[1 - dt > 0 \implies t < \frac{1}{d}\]

Main quantities

\[C(t) = M(1 - dt), \qquad D(t) = M d t, \qquad d(t) = dt\]

Accumulation factor and force of interest

The accumulation factor is a hyperbola:

\[f(t) = \frac{1}{1 - dt}\]

\[M(t) = \frac{C}{1 - dt}, \qquad I(t) = \frac{C dt}{1 - dt}, \qquad i(t) = \frac{dt}{1 - dt}\]

The force of interest increases with \(t\):

\[\delta(t) = \frac{f'(t)}{f(t)} = \frac{d}{1 - dt}\]

It is not constant, so commercial discount is not separable.

Equivalent discount rates

Equal discount factors \(1 - dt = 1 - d_h(ht)\) give proportional rates:

\[d_h = \frac{d}{h}\]

Comparing the three regimes

Fix the same unit interest rate \(i\) for all three. To write commercial discount in terms of \(i\), substitute \(d = i/(1+i)\):

\[f_a(t) = \frac{1+i}{1 - i(t-1)}, \qquad v_a(t) = 1 - \frac{i}{1+i} t\]

Simple Compound Commercial
Accumulation \(f(t)\) \(1 + it\) (line) \((1+i)^t\) (exponential) \(\frac{1+i}{1-i(t-1)}\) (hyperbola)
Discount \(v(t)\) \(\frac{1}{1+it}\) (hyperbola) \((1+i)^{-t}\) (exponential) \(1 - \frac{i}{1+i}t\) (line)
Force \(\delta(t)\) decreasing constant increasing

All accumulation curves pass through \((0, 1)\) and \((1, 1+i)\); all discount curves through \((0, 1)\) and \((1, \frac{1}{1+i})\). Between the crossings the order flips:

  • \(0 < t < 1\): \(f_s > f_c > f_a\) — simple pays most.
  • \(t > 1\): \(f_a > f_c > f_s\) — commercial pays most (until it explodes at \(t = 1/d\)).

Move \(i\) and the date \(t\); switch between the accumulation and the discount view.

Variable rates and average rates

When the rate changes across sub-intervals, the final amount is computed by applying the chosen regime period by period. Let \(\Delta t_k\) be the length of the \(k\)-th sub-interval.

The average rate (\(i_m\), \(d_m\) or \(\delta_m\)) is the constant rate that, applied over the whole duration, gives the same final amount (or present value).

  • Simple interest — duration-weighted arithmetic mean:

\[i_m = \frac{i_1 \Delta t_1 + i_2 \Delta t_2 + \dots}{\sum \Delta t_k}\]

  • Compound interest — \(1 + i_m\) is the duration-weighted geometric mean of the factors:

\[i_m = \left[ (1+i_1)^{\Delta t_1} (1+i_2)^{\Delta t_2} \cdots \right]^{1 / \sum \Delta t_k} - 1\]

  • Force of interest — duration-weighted arithmetic mean:

\[\delta_m = \frac{\delta_1 \Delta t_1 + \delta_2 \Delta t_2 + \dots}{\sum \Delta t_k}\]

  • Commercial discount — duration-weighted arithmetic mean:

\[d_m = \frac{d_1 \Delta t_1 + d_2 \Delta t_2 + \dots}{\sum \Delta t_k}\]

Key takeaways

  • Commercial discount: \(v(t) = 1 - dt\), valid only for \(t < 1/d\).
  • \(f(t) = 1/(1 - dt)\), \(\delta(t) = d/(1-dt)\) increasing, not separable; \(d_h = d/h\).
  • Same \(i\): all \(f\) cross at \(t = 0\) and \(t = 1\); simple wins before 1, commercial after 1, compound sits between.
  • Average rates: arithmetic means for \(i\) (simple), \(d\) and \(\delta\); geometric mean of \(1+i\) for compound.
