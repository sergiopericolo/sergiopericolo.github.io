Capital Accumulation and Loan Amortization

Lectures 7–9 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). Two mirror problems: building a sum by saving, and repaying a sum that was borrowed. Compound interest throughout.

Roadmap:

  1. Accumulation plans — the payment that builds a target sum, the accumulation fund.
  2. Amortization — instalment = principal + interest; outstanding balance.
  3. Closing conditions — three equivalent ways to check a plan.
  4. French, Italian and American plans.
  5. Early repayment — when it pays to close a loan.

Accumulation plans

Goal: hold a sum \(M\) at a future date \(T\).

  • One deposit now: discount the target, \(C = M(1+i)^{-T}\).
  • Constant ordinary deposits for \(n = T\) periods: from \(M = R \cdot s_{\overline{n}|i}\),

\[R = \frac{M}{s_{\overline{n}|i}}\]

Accumulation fund

The fund \(F_t\) is the capital built up to time \(t\). Two equivalent ways to compute it:

  1. Retrospective: accumulate the deposits made up to \(t\). With one initial deposit, \(F_t = C f(t)\).
  2. Prospective: discount the target \(M\) from \(T\) back to \(t\):

\[F_t = M(1+i)^{-(T-t)}\]

Loan amortization

A borrower receives \(S\) at \(t_0 = 0\) and repays it with payments \(R_1, \dots, R_n\) at \(t_1, \dots, t_n\). Three repayment types:

  1. Elementary loan: principal and interest in one payment at maturity.
  2. Interest-only: periodic interest, full principal at maturity.
  3. Gradual amortization: principal and interest spread over the dates.

The quantities of a gradual plan

Each instalment splits into a principal part and an interest part:

\[R_k = C_k + I_k\]

  • \(C_k\): principal repaid (quota capitale).
  • \(I_k\): interest paid (quota interessi).
  • \(E_k\): extinguished debt, the principal repaid so far.
  • \(D_k\): outstanding balance (debito residuo), the principal still owed.

All measured just after the payment at \(t_k\):

\[E_k = S - D_k, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{n} C_k = S\]

\(E_k\) \(D_k\)
Start \(0\) \(S\)
Date \(t_k\) \(\sum_{j \le k} C_j\) \(\sum_{j > k} C_j\)
End \(S\) \(0\)

The balance falls by the principal repaid; interest accrues on the previous balance:

\[D_k = D_{k-1} - C_k, \qquad I_k = D_{k-1} \left[ (1+i)^{t_k - t_{k-1}} - 1 \right]\]

With unit periods, \(I_k = i D_{k-1}\). Substituting \(C_k = R_k - I_k\):

\[D_k = D_{k-1}(1+i)^{t_k - t_{k-1}} - R_k\]

The new balance is the old one accumulated for one period, minus the instalment.

Closing conditions

Discount the recursion and sum over all periods. A plan is correct if any one of these equivalent conditions holds:

  1. Initial: the present value of the instalments equals the loan.

\[S = \sum_{k=1}^{m} R_k (1+i)^{-(t_k - t_0)}\]

  1. Final: the accumulated instalments equal the accumulated loan.

\[S(1+i)^{t_m - t_0} = \sum_{k=1}^{m} R_k (1+i)^{t_m - t_k}\]

  1. Intermediate: the balance at \(t_k\) equals the present value of the instalments still due.

\[D_k = \sum_{j=k+1}^{m} R_j (1+i)^{-(t_j - t_k)}\]

French amortization (constant instalments)

\(R_k = R\) for every \(k\). The initial closing condition gives

\[R = \frac{S}{a_{\overline{m}|i}}, \qquad D_k = R \cdot a_{\overline{m-k}|i}\]

\[I_k = i D_{k-1} = R[1 - (1+i)^{-(m-k+1)}], \qquad C_k = R(1+i)^{-(m-k+1)}\]

Principal parts grow geometrically, interest parts shrink:

\[C_k = C_{k-1}(1+i) = C_1(1+i)^{k-1}\]

Italian amortization (constant principal)

\(C_k = C\) for every \(k\):

\[C = \frac{S}{m}, \qquad D_k = S \frac{m-k}{m}, \qquad I_k = i S \frac{m-k+1}{m}\]

The balance, the interest and so the instalment \(R_k = C + I_k\) all fall linearly.

American amortization (two rates)

Two different rates:

  • \(i\): loan rate, paid on the debt.
  • \(j\): accumulation rate, earned on a savings fund.

Two parallel operations:

  • Interest: each period the borrower pays interest on the whole loan, \(I_k = S i\). The debt to the lender stays \(S\) until maturity.
  • Fund: each period the borrower deposits \(A\) in a fund at rate \(j\), so that the fund equals \(S\) at \(m\):

\[A = \frac{S}{s_{\overline{m}|j}}, \qquad F_k = A \cdot s_{\overline{k}|j}, \qquad D_k = S - F_k\]

Here \(D_k\) is the loan net of the fund. The total outlay per period is

\[R = S i + \frac{S}{s_{\overline{m}|j}}\]

If \(i = j\), this equals the French instalment \(S / a_{\overline{m}|i}\). If \(j < i\) the American plan costs more.

Early repayment

The borrower may close the loan at a date \(t\) between \(t_k\) and \(t_{k+1}\). The payoff value \(V_j(t)\) is the present value of the remaining instalments at a valuation rate \(j\), which may differ from the loan rate \(i\):

\[V_j(t) = \sum_{s=k+1}^{m} R_s (1+j)^{-(t_s - t)}\]

If \(j = i\), the payoff value equals the outstanding balance. Splitting each instalment:

\[V_j(t) = \underbrace{\sum_{s=k+1}^{m} I_s (1+j)^{-(t_s - t)}}_{\text{usufruct}} + \underbrace{\sum_{s=k+1}^{m} C_s (1+j)^{-(t_s - t)}}_{\text{bare ownership}}\]

The usufruct is the value of the future interest; the bare ownership is the value of the future principal.

Compare \(V_j(t)\) with the balance \(D_t\) at rate \(i\):

Payoff vs. balance Close early?
\(j < i\) \(V_j(t) > D_t\) no
\(j > i\) \(V_j(t) < D_t\) yes
\(j = i\) \(V_j(t) = D_t\) indifferent

Key takeaways

  • Accumulation: \(R = M / s_{\overline{n}|i}\); fund retrospective or prospective \(F_t = M(1+i)^{-(T-t)}\).
  • \(R_k = C_k + I_k\), \(D_k = D_{k-1} - C_k\), \(I_k = i D_{k-1}\), \(\sum C_k = S\).
  • Closing: PV of instalments \(= S\); equivalently final or intermediate conditions.
  • French: \(R = S/a_{\overline{m}|i}\), \(C_k\) grows by \((1+i)\). Italian: \(C = S/m\), all falls linearly.
  • American: \(R = Si + S/s_{\overline{m}|j}\); equals French when \(i = j\).
  • Early repayment pays off when the valuation rate \(j\) exceeds the loan rate \(i\).
