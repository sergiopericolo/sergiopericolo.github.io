Choosing Between Financial Operations

Lectures 9–10 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). Two projects, two cash-flow streams: which is better? Two criteria — net present value and internal rate of return — and the cost indicators of a loan.

Roadmap:

  1. Investments and financings — the setting.
  2. NPV (REA, VAN) — definition, rule, additivity.
  3. IRR (TIR) — definition, rule, multiple roots.
  4. The NPV function — why a pure investment has exactly one IRR.
  5. Norström's theorem — a sufficient condition for a unique IRR.
  6. TAN, TAE, TAEG — the cost of a loan.

Investments and financings

Projects are cash-flow vectors \(X = \{(X_k, t_k)\}\) and \(Y = \{(Y_k, t_k)\}\).

  • Investment (strict sense): all outflows come before all inflows.
  • Financing (strict sense): all inflows come before all outflows.

Only homogeneous operations can be compared: both investments or both financings.

Net present value (NPV)

The net present value — in Italian Rendimento Economico Attualizzato (REA) or Valore Attuale Netto (VAN) — is the sum of all discounted flows at a rate \(i > 0\) chosen by the decision maker:

\[NPV_X(i) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} X_k (1+i)^{-(t_k - t_0)}\]

  • Accept \(X\) if \(NPV_X(i) > 0\).
  • Prefer \(X\) to \(Y\) if \(NPV_X(i) > NPV_Y(i)\).

NPV is additive: \(NPV_{X+Y}(i) = NPV_X(i) + NPV_Y(i)\).

Internal rate of return (IRR)

The internal rate of return (Tasso Interno di Rendimento, TIR) is the rate \(i^*\) that makes the NPV zero:

\[\sum_{k=0}^{m} X_k (1+i^*)^{-(t_k - t_0)} = 0\]

  • Investment: \(i^*\) is the return on capital. Prefer the higher IRR.
  • Financing: \(i^*\) is the cost of capital. Prefer the lower IRR.

The weak point. With \(m\) periods the equation is a polynomial of degree \(m\) in \((1+i)^{-1}\). By the fundamental theorem of algebra it has \(m\) complex roots, and there may be more than one real positive root. Then the IRR is ambiguous.

The NPV function of a pure investment

Write \(W(i) = \sum_{k=0}^{m} X_k (1+i)^{-t_k}\) with \(X_0 < 0\) and \(X_k > 0\) for \(k \ge 1\). For \(i > 0\):

  1. Intercept: \(W(0) = \sum_k X_k\). For a sensible investment this is positive.
  2. Asymptote: \(\lim_{i \to +\infty} W(i) = X_0 < 0\).
  3. Decreasing: every term of \(W'(i) = \sum_{k \ge 1} -t_k X_k (1+i)^{-t_k-1}\) is negative.
  4. Convex: every term of \(W''(i) = \sum_{k \ge 1} t_k (t_k+1) X_k (1+i)^{-t_k-2}\) is positive.

A continuous, strictly decreasing function going from a positive value to a negative one crosses zero exactly once (intermediate value theorem). That crossing is the IRR \(i^*\):

  • \(0 < i < i^*\): \(W(i) > 0\), the investment is worth doing.
  • \(i > i^*\): \(W(i) < 0\), it is not.

The default flows are a pure investment. Try [-100, 230, -132]: two sign changes, two IRRs (10% and 20%).

Norström's theorem

A sufficient condition for a unique IRR, also for operations that are not pure investments or financings. Take \(X_0 \neq 0\) and the cumulative sums

\[S_k = \sum_{h=0}^{k} X_h, \qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, m\]

Theorem (Norström). If the sequence \(S_0, S_1, \dots, S_m\) changes sign exactly once (negative to positive for an investment, positive to negative for a financing), the IRR exists and is unique.

The condition is sufficient, not necessary.

Loan cost indicators: TAN, TAE, TAEG

  • TAN (Tasso Annuo Nominale): the pure annual rate of the loan. It is the nominal rate \(J_h\): no compounding within the year, no fees.
  • TAE (Tasso Annuo Effettivo): the effective annual rate from compounding the TAN \(h\) times a year:

\[TAE = \left(1 + \frac{TAN}{h}\right)^h - 1\]

  • TAEG (Tasso Annuo Effettivo Globale), also ISC (Indicatore Sintetico di Costo): the total cost of credit for the consumer. It is the rate that equates the amount received, net of upfront fees, to the present value of all instalments plus periodic fees:

\[V_0 - F_0 = \sum_{k=1}^{m} (R_k + F_k)(1 + TAEG)^{-t_k}\]

where \(V_0\) is the gross loan, \(F_0\) the upfront fees (application, appraisal), \(R_k\) the instalments and \(F_k\) the periodic fees (collection, insurance).

Because of compounding and fees:

\[TAN < TAE < TAEG\]

Key takeaways

  • Compare only homogeneous operations (both investments or both financings).
  • NPV: accept if positive, prefer the larger; additive.
  • IRR: NPV\((i^*) = 0\); higher is better for investments, lower for financings; may not be unique.
  • Pure investment: NPV decreasing and convex, from \(\sum X_k > 0\) to \(X_0 < 0\) → one IRR.
  • Norström: cumulative sums change sign once → unique IRR.
  • TAN (nominal) < TAE (compounded) < TAEG (compounded + fees).
