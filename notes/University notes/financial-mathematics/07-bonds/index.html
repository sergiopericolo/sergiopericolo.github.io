Bonds

Lectures 10–11 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). A bond is a slice of debt that an issuer sells to the market. This note covers its features, its price between coupon dates, and how to measure its return before and after the fact.

Roadmap:

  1. Features of a bond — face value, issue price, redemption, maturity.
  2. Coupon rate and current yield (TRI) — with taxes.
  3. Secondary market — accrued interest, clean and dirty price.
  4. Types of bond.
  5. Ex-post return — what the investor actually earned.
  6. Ex-ante return — current yield and yield to maturity (TIR).

Features of a bond

Companies and public bodies issue bonds to borrow from the market.

  • Face (nominal) value \(C\): the amount on which coupons are computed, usually repaid at maturity.
  • Issue price \(C'\): paid by the buyer at issue \(t_0\).
    • At par: \(C' = C\).
    • Above par: \(C' > C\). The gap is a premium paid by the buyer (aggio for the issuer).
    • Below par: \(C' < C\). The gap is a discount earned by the buyer (disaggio for the issuer).
  • Redemption value \(C''\): paid to the holder at maturity \(\hat t\); often \(C'' = C\).
  • Maturity \(\hat t\): repayment date. The life of the bond is \(\hat t - t_0\).

Two main families:

  1. Zero-coupon bonds (ZCB): no coupons; the return is only redemption minus price.
  2. Coupon bonds: periodic interest payments, the coupons.

Coupon rate and current yield

The coupon is computed on face value with the coupon rate: \(i_h\) per period (or \(j_h\) as a nominal annual rate):

\[\text{coupon} = C \cdot i_h\]

Returns are taxed at rate \(\alpha\). The investor receives the net coupon:

\[\text{net coupon} = C i_h (1 - \alpha)\]

The current yield (Tasso di Rendimento Immediato, TRI) is a short-run measure: net coupon over price. It refers to one coupon period; compound it for the annual figure:

\[TRI_{\text{period}} = \frac{C i_h (1 - \alpha)}{C'}, \qquad TRI_{\text{annual}} = (1 + TRI_{\text{period}})^h - 1\]

Secondary market: accrued interest

On the secondary market bonds already issued are traded. The price must account for the time since the last coupon.

The accrued interest (rateo) is the part of the next coupon already earned since the last coupon date \(t_{k-1}\), up to the trade date \(t\) (with \(t_{k-1} < t < t_k\)). It grows linearly with time:

\[\text{accrued} = \text{coupon} \cdot \frac{t - t_{k-1}}{t_k - t_{k-1}}\]

  • Dirty price (corso tel quel): the price actually paid, accrued interest included.
  • Clean price (corso secco): the quoted price, without accrued interest.

\[\text{dirty price} = \text{clean price} + \text{accrued interest}\]

A bond traded just before a coupon date without the right to that coupon is ex-coupon.

Measuring return: ex-ante and ex-post

  1. Ex-ante: measured at the start \(t\) of the investment. It needs an assumed end date, which may turn out different.
  2. Ex-post: measured after the end. It is the return actually earned between two past dates.

Ex-post return

An investment starts at \(t\) and ends at \(h\), with \(t_0 \le t < h \le \hat t\). Let \(P(X, t)\) be the price paid and \(P(X, h)\) the price at \(h\).

The holding period yield over the whole period is

\[r = \frac{P(X,h) - P(X,t)}{P(X,t)}\]

To compare investments of different length, annualize. Let \(V(X, h)\) be the final value at \(h\): for a ZCB, \(V(X,h) = P(X,h)\); for a coupon bond, also add the coupons received and reinvested up to \(h\).

\[r_c = \left( \frac{V(X,h)}{P(X,t)} \right)^{1/(h-t)} - 1 \quad \text{(compound)}\]

\[r_s = \frac{V(X,h) - P(X,t)}{(h-t) P(X,t)} \quad \text{(simple)}\]

Ex-ante return: TRI and TIR

The current yield on the secondary market uses the clean price:

\[TRI = \frac{\text{coupon}}{\text{clean price}}\]

The yield to maturity (TIR) is the rate \(y\) that equates the dirty price at \(t\) to the present value of all future flows \(x_h\):

\[P(X,t) = \sum_{h=1}^{m} \frac{x_h}{(1+y)^{t_h - t}}\]

For the net TIR, use net coupons and a net dirty price: clean price + net accrued interest (accrued \(\cdot (1 - \alpha)\)).

The yield to maturity has two built-in limits:

  1. It assumes the investor holds the bond until maturity.
  2. It assumes every coupon is reinvested at the same rate \(y\).

Key takeaways

  • Face value \(C\), issue price \(C'\) (par, above, below), redemption \(C''\), maturity \(\hat t\).
  • Coupon \(= C i_h\), net coupon \(= C i_h (1-\alpha)\); TRI = net coupon / price, annualized by compounding.
  • Dirty price = clean price + accrued interest; accrued interest is linear between coupon dates.
  • Ex-post: \(r = (P_h - P_t)/P_t\); annualize with final value \(V\) (coupons reinvested).
  • Yield to maturity: rate that discounts all flows to the dirty price; assumes hold-to-maturity and reinvestment at \(y\).
