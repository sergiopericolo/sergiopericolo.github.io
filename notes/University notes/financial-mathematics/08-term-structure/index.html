The Term Structure of Interest Rates

The Term Structure of Interest Rates

Lecture 12 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). So far one rate discounted every date. In real markets each maturity has its own price. This note builds the term structure from zero-coupon prices, derives forward prices and rates by no-arbitrage, and links them to the yield of a bond.

Roadmap:

  1. Spot prices — the market assumptions and no-arbitrage.
  2. Forward prices — locking in today a future investment.
  3. Bond prices and the term structure.
  4. Spot and forward rates.
  5. Generalized NPV.

Spot prices

How does the market price fixed-income securities? Three assumptions:

  1. The market lists unit zero-coupon bonds (pay 1 euro) for maturities \(0 \le t_1 \le t_2 \le \dots \le t_m\).
  2. They are always available and infinitely divisible.
  3. The market is frictionless: no transaction costs, no taxes.

Under these assumptions no-arbitrage holds: no riskless profit without an initial investment.

\(v(t, t_h)\) is the price agreed at \(t\) for a unit ZCB maturing at \(t_h \ge t\): the spot price. It is a discount factor:

\[v(t_h, t_h) = 1, \qquad v(t, +\infty) = 0\]

No-arbitrage forces spot prices to fall strictly with maturity:

\[v(t, t_h) > v(t, t_{h+1}), \qquad h = 1, \dots, m-1\]

Forward prices

An investor at \(t\) wants to fix today the price of an investment from \(s\) to \(\tau\), with \(t < s < \tau\). A synthetic strategy at \(t\):

  1. Buy one ZCB maturing at \(\tau\): pay \(v(t, \tau)\), receive 1 at \(\tau\).
  2. Short sell \(v(t,\tau)/v(t,s)\) ZCBs maturing at \(s\): receive \(v(t, \tau)\) now.
Date Flow
\(t\) \(-v(t,\tau) + v(t,\tau) = 0\)
\(s\) \(-v(t,\tau)/v(t,s)\)
\(\tau\) \(+1\)

Net flow at \(t\) is zero: the investor has fixed at \(t\) an investment of \(v(t,\tau)/v(t,s)\) at \(s\) that returns 1 at \(\tau\). This ratio is the forward price:

\[v(t, s, \tau) = \frac{v(t, \tau)}{v(t, s)}, \qquad 0 \le t \le s \le \tau\]

It is the discount factor agreed at \(t\) for money moved from \(\tau\) back to \(s\). If \(s = t\) it is the spot price: \(v(t, t, \tau) = v(t, \tau)\).

Bond prices and the term structure

A bond \(X\) pays \(X_h\) at \(t_h\). Its dirty price can be written with its yield \(i^*\):

\[P(X,t) = \sum_{h=1}^{m} X_h (1+i^*)^{-(t_h - t)}\]

By no-arbitrage the bond equals a portfolio of \(X_h\) unit ZCBs maturing at each \(t_h\). So its price must also be

\[P(X,t) = \sum_{h=1}^{m} X_h \cdot v(t, t_h)\]

This is the basic link between bond prices and the term structure. The yield \(i^*\) is a single rate that summarizes all the spot rates the bond's flows face.

Spot and forward rates

Turn prices into compound rates. The spot rate \(h(t, s)\) for an investment from \(t\) to \(s\):

\[v(t, s)[1 + h(t, s)]^{s-t} = 1 \implies h(t, s) = v(t, s)^{-1/(s-t)} - 1\]

The forward rate \(h(t, s, \tau)\), agreed at \(t\) for an investment from \(s\) to \(\tau\):

\[v(t, s, \tau)[1 + h(t, s, \tau)]^{\tau-s} = 1 \implies h(t, s, \tau) = v(t, s, \tau)^{-1/(\tau-s)} - 1\]

Substituting the forward price gives forward rates straight from spot prices:

\[h(t, s, \tau) = \left[ \frac{v(t, s)}{v(t, \tau)} \right]^{1/(\tau-s)} - 1\]

The function \(h(t, s, \tau)\), \(0 \le t < s < \tau\), is the term structure of interest rates at \(t\).

Generalized NPV

When the curve is not flat (spot rates depend on maturity), NPV must discount each flow at its own spot rate. The generalized NPV (VANG) at \(t = 0\):

\[VANG = \sum_{k=1}^{m} X_k [1 + h(0, t_k)]^{-t_k}\]

The IRR is still the single constant rate \(i^*\) that discounts the flows to the initial price.

Key takeaways

  • Assumptions: unit ZCBs for every maturity, divisible, no frictions → no-arbitrage.
  • Spot prices \(v(t, t_h)\): \(v(t_h,t_h) = 1\), strictly decreasing in maturity.
  • Forward price \(v(t,s,\tau) = v(t,\tau)/v(t,s)\), built by buying the long ZCB and shorting the short one.
  • A bond is a portfolio of ZCBs: \(P = \sum X_h v(t, t_h)\).
  • Spot rate \(h(t,s) = v(t,s)^{-1/(s-t)} - 1\); forward rate \(h(t,s,\tau) = [v(t,s)/v(t,\tau)]^{1/(\tau-s)} - 1\).
  • VANG discounts each flow at its own spot rate.
