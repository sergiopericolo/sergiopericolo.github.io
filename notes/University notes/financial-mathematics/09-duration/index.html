en

Time Indices and Duration

Lecture 13 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). One number to describe when a stream of payments arrives on average — and how much its price moves when rates move. The key tool is the duration.

Roadmap:

  1. Maturity and time to maturity.
  2. Arithmetic mean maturity — weights are the amounts.
  3. Financial mean maturity — one payment worth the same.
  4. Duration — weights are the present values.
  5. Duration and price risk — modified duration.

Maturity and time to maturity

A stream pays \(R_1, \dots, R_m \ge 0\) at \(t_1, \dots, t_m\), valued at \(t\).

  • Maturity: the date of the last payment, \(t_m\).
  • Time to maturity (residual life): \(t_m - t\).

At \(t = 0\) the two coincide.

Arithmetic mean maturity

The amount-weighted average of the times to payment, valued at \(t\) with \(0 \le t \le t_1\):

\[\bar{t} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{m} R_k (t_k - t)}{\sum_{k=1}^{m} R_k}\]

It is purely accounting: it does not depend on any rate or regime.

Financial mean maturity

The financial mean maturity \(z\) is the single date at which one payment equal to the total \(\sum R_i\) has the same present value as the whole stream:

\[\sum_{i=1}^{m} R_i v(t_i) = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{m} R_i \right) v(z)\]

\(z\) depends on the regime. Under commercial discount, \(v(t) = 1 - dt\) is linear, so

\[z = \bar{t}\]

Duration

The duration (durata media finanziaria) \(D\) is set under compound interest at rate \(i > 0\), with a flat curve. The dirty price is \(P(i) = \sum_{h=1}^{m} R_h (1+i)^{-t_h}\). The duration is the average of the payment dates, weighted by present values:

\[D = \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{m} t_h R_h (1+i)^{-t_h}}{P(i)}\]

With a non-flat curve, use the spot prices \(v(0, t_h)\):

\[D = \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{m} t_h R_h v(0, t_h)}{\sum_{h=1}^{m} R_h v(0, t_h)}\]

Facts that follow from the definition:

  • A ZCB has \(D\) equal to its maturity.
  • A coupon bond has \(D\) below its maturity: early coupons pull the average forward.
  • Higher coupons or higher rates give shorter duration (more weight on early flows).

Duration and price risk

The price changes with the rate. To first order:

\[\Delta P \approx P'(i) \Delta i, \qquad \frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx \frac{P'(i)}{P(i)} \Delta i\]

Differentiate \(P(i)\):

\[P'(i) = -\sum_{h=1}^{m} t_h R_h (1+i)^{-t_h-1} = -\frac{1}{1+i} \sum_{h=1}^{m} t_h R_h (1+i)^{-t_h}\]

so

\[\frac{\Delta P}{P} \approx -\frac{1}{1+i} \cdot \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{m} t_h R_h (1+i)^{-t_h}}{P(i)} \Delta i = -\frac{D}{1+i} \Delta i\]

\(D^* = \frac{D}{1+i}\) is the modified duration: the approximate percentage price change for a unit change in the rate. Longer duration means more price risk.

The tangent line is the duration approximation; the gap to the curve (convexity) grows with \(|\Delta i|\).

Key takeaways

  • Arithmetic mean maturity \(\bar t\): weights are amounts, no rate involved.
  • Financial mean maturity \(z\): one total payment with equal PV; \(z = \bar t\) under commercial discount.
  • Duration \(D = \sum t_h R_h v^{t_h} / P\): weights are present values.
  • \(\Delta P / P \approx -D^* \Delta i\), with \(D^* = D/(1+i)\).
  • ZCB: \(D\) = maturity. Coupons and higher rates shorten \(D\).
