Risk, Life Tables and Survival Models

en

Risk, Life Tables and Survival Models

Lectures 13–14 of Financial Mathematics (University of Brescia). From certain amounts to random ones. Actuarial mathematics values operations whose amounts or dates depend on chance — above all, on how long a person lives. This note builds the demographic side: life tables, survival probabilities, life expectancy and the classic survival laws.

Roadmap:

  1. Risk and insurance — uncertainty vs. risk, pure vs. speculative risk.
  2. Life tables — \(l_x\), \(d_x\), \(p_x\), \(q_x\).
  3. Multi-year probabilities — survival, death, deferred death.
  4. Life expectancy \(e_x\).
  5. Force of mortality \(\mu_x\).
  6. Survival laws — constant force, De Moivre, Gompertz–Makeham.

Risk and insurance

Actuarial mathematics studies random financial operations: exchanges over time where amounts and/or dates are random. Its main field is insurance (art. 1882 of the Italian Civil Code): non-life (damage) and life insurance.

  • Uncertainty (not measurable): we cannot assign probabilities to the future scenarios.
  • Risk (measurable uncertainty): we can assign a probability distribution.

Risks split into:

  • Speculative (financial) risks: deviations from the expected value, either gains or losses.
  • Pure risks: only losses, when they occur.

Life insurance

In life insurance the insurer pays a capital or an annuity on an event tied to the insured's life (reaching an age, death), in exchange for a premium. Its roles: social security, saving, investment. Valuation rests on a technical basis:

  1. Financial assumption: the interest rate.
  2. Demographic assumption: probabilities of survival and death.

Life tables

Follow a cohort of people born in the same year:

  • \(l_0\): number of newborns.
  • \(l_x\): survivors at age \(x\). The map \(x \mapsto l_x\) is the survival function. The limiting age \(\omega\) satisfies \(l_\omega = 0\), \(l_{\omega - 1} > 0\).
  • \(d_x = l_x - l_{x+1}\): deaths between ages \(x\) and \(x+1\).

One-year rates:

\[p_x = \frac{l_{x+1}}{l_x} \quad \text{(survival)}, \qquad q_x = \frac{d_x}{l_x} = 1 - p_x \quad \text{(mortality)}\]

Multi-year probabilities

Symbol Meaning Formula
\({}_n p_x\) a person aged \(x\) survives \(n\) more years \(\frac{l_{x+n}}{l_x}\)
\({}_{/n} q_x\) dies within \(n\) years \(\frac{l_x - l_{x+n}}{l_x} = 1 - {}_n p_x\)
\({}_{m/n} q_x\) dies between ages \(x+m\) and \(x+m+n\) \(\frac{l_{x+m} - l_{x+m+n}}{l_x}\)
\({}_{m/1} q_x\) dies in the year after age \(x+m\) \(\frac{d_{x+m}}{l_x}\)

Two probability rules hold:

  1. Compound probability (survive each year in turn):

\[{}_n p_x = p_x \cdot p_{x+1} \cdots p_{x+n-1}\]

  1. Total probability (die in the first year, or survive it and die in the second, …):

\[{}_{/n} q_x = q_x + p_x q_{x+1} + {}_2 p_x q_{x+2} + \dots + {}_{n-1} p_x q_{x+n-1}\]

Life expectancy

Let \(x\) be the current age. The random variable is the number of whole future years lived, \(h = 0, 1, \dots, \omega - x - 1\). The probability of living exactly \(h\) more whole years (dying between \(x+h\) and \(x+h+1\)) is

\[P(h) = {}_{h/1} q_x = \frac{l_{x+h} - l_{x+h+1}}{l_x}\]

The curtate life expectancy \(e_x\) is the expected value:

\[e_x = \sum_{h=0}^{\omega-x-1} h \cdot {}_{h/1} q_x\]

Expanding the sum and cancelling terms gives the practical form

\[e_x = \frac{\sum_{h=1}^{\omega-x-1} l_{x+h}}{l_x}\]

and a recursion for computing backwards from old ages:

\[e_x = p_x (1 + e_{x+1})\]

\(e_0 < \omega\). Someone who has already reached age \(x\) expects to die at age \(x + e_x\), which is usually above \(e_0\): they have already survived the early risks.

Force of mortality

For a continuous view, the probability that a person aged \(x\) dies in a short interval \(\Delta x\) is about proportional to \(\Delta x\):

\[{}_{/\Delta x} q_x \approx \mu_x \Delta x\]

Letting \(\Delta x \to 0\) gives the force of mortality:

\[\mu_x = -\frac{l'_x}{l_x} = -\frac{d}{dx} \ln l_x\]

It plays the role \(\delta\) played for interest. Integrating:

\[l_x = l_a e^{-\int_a^x \mu_s ds}, \qquad {}_n p_x = e^{-\int_x^{x+n} \mu_s ds}\]

Survival laws

Analytic forms for \(\mu_x\) give analytic life tables.

  1. Constant force: \(\mu_x = k\). Then \(l_x = l_a e^{-k(x-a)}\) and \({}_n p_x = e^{-kn}\) depends only on \(n\), not on age. Unrealistic for people, but simple.

  2. De Moivre (1725): the same number of deaths at every age. \(l_x\) falls linearly:

\[l_x = l_a \frac{\omega - x}{\omega - a}, \qquad \mu_x = \frac{1}{\omega - x}\]

  1. Gompertz–Makeham: the most realistic and widely used. The force of mortality has a constant part (accidents) and a part growing exponentially with age (ageing):

\[\mu_x = \alpha + \beta c^x, \quad c > 1, \qquad l_x = k s^x g^{c^x}\]

With \(\alpha = 0\) (so \(s = 1\)) it is the pure Gompertz law.

Key takeaways

  • Risk is measurable uncertainty; pure risks only cause losses.
  • Technical basis = financial assumption + demographic assumption.
  • \(d_x = l_x - l_{x+1}\), \(p_x = l_{x+1}/l_x\), \(q_x = 1 - p_x\), \({}_n p_x = l_{x+n}/l_x\).
  • Compound and total probability rules link one-year and multi-year rates.
  • \(e_x = \sum_{h \ge 1} l_{x+h} / l_x\), and \(e_x = p_x(1 + e_{x+1})\).
  • \(\mu_x = -l'_x / l_x\), \({}_n p_x = e^{-\int \mu}\).
  • Laws: constant force (age-free), De Moivre (linear \(l_x\)), Gompertz–Makeham (\(\alpha + \beta c^x\)).
